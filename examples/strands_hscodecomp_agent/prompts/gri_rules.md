## General Rules of Interpretation (GRI) for Product Classification

The following six rules must be applied in order, from Rule 1 to Rule 6, without skipping any rule.

### Rule 1: Heading texts and notes take precedence

Classification shall first be determined according to the terms of the headings (4/6-digit HS codes) and any relevant section and chapter notes.
The subsequent rules apply only when the heading texts and notes do not determine the classification.

### Rule 2: Incomplete articles and extension to materials

Rule 2(a): An incomplete or unfinished article (e.g., a bicycle without its wheels) shall be classified as the complete article if it already has the essential character of the complete article.
Rule 2(b): An article made of a given material or substance that retains its original character after other materials or substances are added (e.g., a plastic cup with a metal base) shall be classified according to the original material.
Example: An unassembled computer motherboard (already having the function of a motherboard) → heading 8473 (parts of computers).

### Rule 3: Decision logic when a product can be classified under multiple headings

When a product can be classified under two or more headings, determine the classification in the following order of priority:

1. **Most specific description first**: A more specific description takes precedence over a more general one;
2. **Essential character decides**: Classify according to the essential character determined by the product's principal material, function, or use;
3. **Last in numerical order**: If the classification still cannot be determined, classify under the heading that occurs last in numerical order.

Example: An electric toothbrush (with attributes of both a "household appliance" and an "oral hygiene tool") → under the most specific description, heading 8509 (electromechanical domestic appliances), not heading 9603 (toothbrushes).

### Rule 4: Most akin by analogy

When a product cannot be classified under the three rules above, it shall be classified under the heading appropriate to the goods to which it is most akin.
Example: Imitation leather made of a new material (not specified in the HS) → by analogy, heading 3921 (artificial leather).

### Rule 5: Packing materials and containers

Rule 5(a): Packing materials or containers presented with the product (e.g., a jewelry box) shall be classified with the product if they are normally sold with it; otherwise they are classified separately.
Rule 5(b): Reusable packing containers (e.g., metal gas cylinders) shall be classified separately.
Example: A glass bottle containing perfume → perfume heading 3303; a glass bottle sold separately → heading 7010.

### Rule 6: Subheading classification level by level

Classification in subheadings (6-digit and further HS codes) shall be determined level by level: first determine the first-level subheading (digits 5–6), then the lower-level subheadings in turn. Each level of classification must take into account the relevant subheading notes and the relationships among subheadings at the same level.
Example: After classifying a product under heading 6205 (men's shirts), select the subheading by material → 620520 (of cotton) or 620530 (of man-made fibers).
